\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Traditional driver training methods face significant limitations in terms of safety, cost, and scalability, particularly when training on high-end vehicles or in hazardous scenarios. This project presents the design and implementation of DriveXR, a Virtual Reality (VR) driving simulator built to help novice drivers safely practise vehicle control before driving on public roads. The system is developed in Unity 6 and runs as a PC-hosted Windows application, streaming visual output to a Meta Quest 3 headset via Meta Quest Link. This architecture retains full six-degrees-of-freedom head tracking and optical hand tracking from the Quest 3's onboard cameras while leveraging desktop-class GPU rendering, avoiding the platform limitations of a fully standalone deployment. Physical input is provided by a Logitech G29 racing wheel and pedal set connected directly to the PC via USB, offering realistic steering, throttle, and braking control substantially more immersive than gamepad or keyboard input. A Tata Safari 2024 is used as the demonstration vehicle, modelled with Unity WheelCollider-based physics tuned to approximate real-world handling characteristics. The simulator provides three structured training environments of progressive difficulty: a straight bounded highway for control familiarisation, a winding road for handling practice, and a signalised city road grid populated with AI-controlled traffic for advanced scenario training. The NPC traffic system uses a state-machine-based controller incorporating obstacle avoidance, traffic light compliance, and intersection yielding behaviour. VR hand tracking enables direct in-cockpit interaction with the steering wheel and horn. Preliminary testing with the supervising faculty and additional participants identified key usability factors including simulator sickness adaptation patterns in VR-inexperienced users, the influence of physical hardware ergonomics on control legibility, and headset height calibration requirements. This report presents the literature review, system architecture, implementation details, and preliminary evaluation results that document the development and validation of DriveXR as an accessible, affordable driving practice platform.
